LyrionPlay

A bit-perfect Lyrion Music Server player for Apple Silicon Macs, built on squeezelite.

v0.4.14 Apple Silicon macOS 14+ Bit-perfect DSD over DoP
Download latest Installation

LyrionPlay plays music from your Lyrion Music Server (LMS) on your Mac. LMS sees it as an ordinary squeezelite player. It lives in your menu bar, and its player runs in the background.

Features

Bit-perfect on a DAC

Exclusive (hog) mode, with the DAC switched to each track's sample rate. Samples reach the DAC unchanged at full volume.

Told about your DAC

LMS learns your DAC's highest sample rate and transcodes anything above it. Rates the DAC lacks below that are resampled in the player, only when needed.

DSD

Sent as DoP to DACs you switch DoP on for (macOS can't tell which accept it), and converted to PCM for the rest.

LMS replay gain

Applied by the player, exactly as LMS sends it.

Volume, your choice per device

Device's own volume: the LMS slider moves your Mac's volume or your DAC's hardware volume, and a change made on the Mac moves the LMS slider (the volume keys when the output is the Mac's built-in speakers or Follow the macOS output, with Exclusive mode off). Software, where the Mac can't set the device's volume: the player scales the samples. Fixed, on a DAC: LyrionPlay sets LMS's fixed output level for you; a DAC whose volume the Mac sets holds the level LMS had, and on any other you use the DAC's or amplifier's own volume.

Menu bar app

Switch the player on and off, see what it's doing (the server, the device and the rate it plays at, the DSD rate for DoP), and choose the output device and its options.

Signed by you

Every user signs their own copy with a certificate made on their own Mac, so no Apple developer account is involved.

Requirements

  • A Mac with Apple Silicon (M1 or later), running macOS 14 Sonoma or later.
  • Lyrion Music Server on your network.

Install

Download the latest release, then follow INSTALL.md. It takes one Terminal command.

How it works

part what it does
engine/squeezelite with a native CoreAudio output added (output_coreaudio.c): exclusive mode, per-track rate and format switching, DoP, the device's volume in both directions, and a report of what it plays for the menu.
app/Sources/AgentA background launch agent. It reads the device's abilities from the engine, builds the engine's arguments, keeps the engine running, passes volume changes made on the Mac to LMS, and keeps LMS's fixed-volume setting in step with each device's choice.
app/Sources/AppThe menu bar app: On/Off, status, settings.
scripts/build.sh builds the app and the release. test.sh is the regression suite. install.sh and uninstall.sh are what users run.

Building from source

You need Apple's Command Line Tools (xcode-select --install). Nothing else is installed system-wide.

scripts/build.sh            # build and check
scripts/build.sh --install  # build, then install with your own certificate
scripts/build.sh --release  # build the two release zips (app, codec source) into dist/
scripts/test.sh             # the regression suite on its own

Every build runs the regression suite (scripts/test.sh) and stops if a test fails. It needs no DAC, server or keychain: the CoreAudio output is tested against a fake audio system.

The first build downloads pinned, checksummed sources and compiles them for Apple Silicon: CMake, and the codec libraries (FLAC, Ogg, Vorbis, Opus, mpg123, FAAD2, soxr, ALAC). This takes a while.

Credits

LyrionPlay's audio engine is squeezelite.

  • Adrian Smith wrote it (2012–2015).
  • Ralph Irving has maintained and developed it since 2015, with many contributors.
  • Squeezelite does everything that makes this a real LMS player: the slimproto protocol, decoding, gapless playback, sync groups, resampling, DSD and DoP.
  • LyrionPlay adds a macOS output to it. Every change to squeezelite's own files is listed in engine/UPSTREAM.md.
  • Thank you, Adrian and Ralph.

Squeezelite in turn contains:

  • dsd2pcm by Sebastian Gesemann;
  • the Daphile project's DSD patch.

Also used:

  • Codec libraries: FLAC, Ogg and Vorbis (Xiph.Org), Opus and opusfile, mpg123, FAAD2, soxr, and Apple Lossless (ALAC).
  • The Music Player Daemon's macOS output was a reference for CoreAudio's exclusive mode and format selection.
  • The icon joins the play arrow from Google's Material Symbols (Apache 2.0) to the Lyrion waveform from LMS-Material by Craig Drummond (MIT).
  • Lyrion Music Server and its community.
